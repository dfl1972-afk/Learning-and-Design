Higher Education Active Learning Assessment Alignment

HOTL2007 Facilities Planning and Design

A higher education learning design example focused on helping students connect facilities planning, commercial feasibility and operational design decisions. The design approach emphasised scaffolded learning, collaborative analysis, industry context and activities that prepared students to apply concepts rather than only recall them.

Design problemMake technical planning concepts meaningful for hospitality and hotel management learners.
Design responseUse guided analysis, design prompts, industry examples and assessment-linked activities.
Evidence of capabilityConstructive alignment, active learning, digital resource design and applied assessment preparation.

Example evidence

  • Week-level teaching resources connecting facilities decisions to commercial and operational consequences.
  • Activities prompting learners to analyse site attributes, feasibility, service flow and design trade-offs.
  • Assessment-facing scaffolding that helps students move from concept knowledge to applied planning judgement.
Simulation Experiential Learning Decision-Making

Hotel Management Simulation

An experiential learning design example where learners practise operational decision-making in a simulated hotel environment. The learning experience supports decisions around pricing, staffing, maintenance, marketing and service quality, then encourages reflection on how those decisions affect performance and guest outcomes.

Design problemMove learners from passive content review into practice-based managerial judgement.
Design responseCreate cycles of decision, consequence, feedback and reflection.
Evidence of capabilityExperiential design, scenario logic, feedback loops and authentic workplace transfer.

Example evidence

  • Simulation structure requiring learners to make operational choices rather than simply describe hotel management concepts.
  • Decision areas covering pricing, staffing, service quality, maintenance, marketing and guest experience.
  • Reflection prompts linking simulation outcomes to business reasoning and continuous improvement.
VET Regulated Learning Learner Resources

SITHGAM022 Responsible Gambling

A regulated learning design example focused on translating legal, ethical and harm-minimisation obligations into learner-facing content. The design task was to make compliance expectations clear and practical while supporting real-world judgement in hospitality and gambling environments.

Design problemMake complex legal responsibilities understandable and usable for front-line learners.
Design responseUse plain-language explanations, realistic scenarios, visual cues and decision points.
Evidence of capabilityCompliance translation, learner-centred writing and assessment-ready resource design.

Example evidence

  • Selected learner-guide excerpt explaining intoxication and duty-of-care concepts in plain language.
  • Embedded video and QR prompts to support multimodal engagement and workplace relevance.
  • Scenario-ready explanations that help learners recognise signs, respond appropriately and document decisions.

AI and Data-Informed Learning Design

I use AI and data-informed approaches to support research, learning design, content development, evaluation and workflow improvement. My approach focuses on responsible use, human oversight, critical evaluation of outputs and appropriate integration of AI into authentic learning and workplace capability.

  • AI-assisted research: exploring source material, identifying patterns, testing explanations and strengthening design rationale.
  • Design iteration: using AI to generate options, compare approaches, refine learning activities and improve clarity while retaining human review.
  • Analytics awareness: using evidence, learner needs, feedback and performance indicators to guide design decisions and improvement cycles.
  • Responsible AI: considering transparency, privacy, data quality, bias, assessment integrity and clear boundaries for appropriate learner use.
  • Research assistant experimentation: exploring an in-development AI-supported research workflow for structured notes, retrieval, source organisation, local model testing and human checkpoints.

Responsible AI Practice

AI is treated as a support for professional judgement, not a replacement for it. My focus is on critical review, transparency, appropriate boundaries, academic integrity, accessibility, learner safety and client confidentiality.

Where AI is used in learning design, I consider what the tool is suitable for, what still requires human expertise, and how learners can be supported to use AI ethically and effectively.
